AI social media manager: drafts in your voice, approved by you

Ward Consulting AI · November 10, 2026

An AI social media manager drafts your posts, captions and short video scripts in your own voice, then waits for you to approve each one before anything goes out. For an owner who knows the product but has no spare hours for the feed, that is the real offer: someone writes the first draft every week, and you decide what is said.

What a social media service actually drafts

Most small businesses do not need a dozen platforms or a daily flood of content. They need a steady rhythm of posts that sound like the owner. A good service begins by learning what you sell, what customers keep asking about, and which moments in your week are worth mentioning: a new product, a job you finished, an event you are working, a question that came in twice.

From that, the helper drafts posts, newsletter blurbs and short video scripts for you to review. It suggests. You choose.

Keeping your voice

Voice is the part buyers worry about most, and they are right to. Ask to see drafts before the service runs for a week, and make sure you can say 'more plainspoken' or 'fewer exclamation marks' and see the change in the next batch. Ask how your corrections are saved, so the next draft starts from what you already fixed.

Brand rules matter as much as tone, and a good provider will ask you for them in writing. Words you never use, claims you cannot back up, topics you avoid and customers you never name should be written down before the first draft, not discovered after a post is live.

What you approve before anything posts

In the early months nothing should publish on its own. Every post, reply and promotion waits for your yes. Some things always come to you, whatever the schedule says: anything that mentions a price or an offer, any claim about results, any post that names a customer, and any public answer to a complaint.

Silence is never a yes. If you do not reply to a draft, it stays in the queue, and that is the correct result.

Ask where approval happens. A short message, a shared review page or a weekly list can all work, as long as you see the exact wording that will go out, including links, tags and any photo.

Questions to ask a social media provider

  1. Who writes the drafts, and can I see examples from a business like mine before I commit?
  2. Where does my approval happen, and can I edit a draft before I say yes?
  3. Which accounts can the service reach, and how do I remove that access?
  4. What happens to a comment or message that needs a human reply?
  5. Who owns the drafts and the saved voice notes if I leave?
  6. How do you handle claims, prices and photos of customers?

What to expect in the first month

The first month is mostly calibration. Expect to edit more drafts than you approve untouched. That is normal, and it is part of what you are paying for, because drafts move closer to your voice once you tell the service what was wrong with them.

Expect posts tied to real moments in your business. A good service asks for a short note each week about what happened: a booth at an event, a question a customer asked, a new item on the shelf. Without those notes, a feed drifts into generic posts that could belong to any shop. Watch for that drift.

Expect a daily check. You should see what was drafted, what you approved and what is still waiting. A draft that has sat unanswered is a signal to talk about the schedule, not a reason to approve everything in one batch.

Signs a social media provider is a good fit

  • It asks about your customers and your events before it suggests a single post.
  • It shows you example drafts, including ones it would not publish.
  • It names who approves what, in writing, before you pay.
  • It explains what happens to your accounts and drafts if you stop.

If a provider skips any of those, the feed will probably be fine for a month and then drift. Ask again before you sign.

Common questions

What does an AI social media manager do?

It drafts posts, captions, newsletter blurbs and short video scripts in your voice, based on what you sell and what you tell it. A person approves every draft before it goes out. It should not decide your strategy alone, and it should not reply to customers in public without your okay.

Will AI posts sound like my business?

They can get close, and they improve when you correct them. Expect the first drafts to need edits, and ask any provider how your corrections are saved and reused. You should always have the final say on wording.

Can an AI social media manager post without my approval?

It should not, especially at the start. Set it up so every post, reply and promotion waits for your yes. Silence is never a yes, and anything that mentions a price, a claim or a named customer should always come to you first.
