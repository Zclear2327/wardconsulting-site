Questions to ask before buying AI agents

Ward Consulting AI · November 11, 2026

Before you pay for AI agent help, you need plain answers to a few questions. This AI agent buying guide lists ten questions to ask any provider, explains what a good answer sounds like and points out the warning signs. The questions work for any provider, including us, and they cover control, accounts, data, cost and support.

Why buying AI agents is different from buying software

Software sits on your computer and waits for you to open it. An AI agent reads your mail, drafts messages and acts on your behalf, sometimes while you are busy with customers. That makes the buying decision a question about control. You want to know who approves what, who can see your information, what happens when you stop and what you will actually pay each month. A polished demo answers none of those.

The right way to buy is to ask for the answers before you connect a single account. A provider that is glad to answer is a provider you can hold to its word. A provider that keeps steering back to the demo is telling you something too.

The ten questions to ask

  1. Who approves anything that spends money or cannot be undone? The answer should name the approval step in plain words. "The AI handles it" is not an answer.
  2. Who owns the accounts and the information? You should own your accounts, and you should be able to take your records with you if you leave.
  3. Will you ever ask for my passwords? A trustworthy provider says no. You sign in to your own accounts yourself.
  4. Does the helper say that it is AI? Customers have a right to know when they are talking to a machine, and the helper should say so when asked.
  5. How do I pause or stop everything, and how fast? You want a stop that works in seconds, not a support ticket that takes days.
  6. What exactly does the monthly price include? Ask which agents, which tasks and how many people, and ask for the answer in writing.
  7. What do I pay for separately? Ask about your own AI account, any setup charge and any change you will need later.
  8. What happens in the first month, and who does the work? You should hear about a clear first task and drafts you read before anything goes live. Our pricing guide lists the costs to ask about.
  9. How will I know each day what ran and what is waiting for me? A daily summary is the minimum for a helper that acts for you.
  10. Will the helper give legal, tax or financial advice? A good provider says no and sends those questions to a professional.

Ask the questions in the order above. The first three tell you whether the provider understands control. The rest tell you whether the price and the support match what you expect. If the answer to the first question is vague, stop there, because nothing else the provider says will be easier to trust.

What a good answer sounds like

A good answer is specific, short and written down. It names a person or a step, says what happens when something goes wrong and admits what the helper cannot do. Be wary of answers built on words like smart, automatic or autonomous with nothing behind them. Ask for the answers in an email, so you have them when you need them. Bring the list to every call and do not skip a question because the answer seems obvious. The obvious answers are often the ones that change in the contract.

Notice how the provider handles a question it cannot answer yet. Honest providers say so and promise to find out. Evasive ones change the subject. Both facts are useful when you compare.

Red flags to watch for

  • A promise of results with no way to check them.
  • A request for your passwords or for full access to every account at once.
  • A sales page that talks about the AI and never mentions approvals.
  • No written answer about who owns your data.
  • A contract you must sign before the provider explains what happens on day one.
  • Pressure to decide today.

One red flag is worth a question. Several together are a reason to look elsewhere. If you compare two providers, put the same ten questions to both and compare the written answers, not the sales calls.

Common questions

What should I ask an AI agent provider before I pay?

Ask who approves spending and irreversible actions, who owns the accounts and information, whether they will ever ask for your passwords, how you pause everything, and exactly what the monthly price includes. Ask for the answers in writing.

What is a red flag when buying AI agent services?

Watch for promised results you cannot check, requests for your passwords or full access to every account, no written answer about data ownership and pressure to sign before anything is explained. One warning sign is worth a question, and several together are a reason to look elsewhere.

Do I need my own AI account to use an AI agent service?

Ward Consulting AI says yes. You need your own AI account, paid separately, to use its plans. Ask any provider which accounts and costs sit outside its monthly price before you agree to anything.
