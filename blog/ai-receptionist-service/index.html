AI receptionist service: what to expect before you buy

Ward Consulting AI · November 10, 2026

An AI receptionist service answers the questions and messages your customers send when you cannot pick up, then passes the ones that need a human to you. If you are comparing options, the useful question is not which tool sounds the most impressive. It is what the service covers, what it hands back to you, and who sets it up so it fits the way your business really runs.

What an AI receptionist service should handle

A good receptionist service starts with the questions you already answer every day. For a salon, that might be your hours, the price of common services and whether a chair is open this week. For a contractor, it might be your service area, which jobs you take and when someone can come out for an estimate. The service should answer those from information you give it, and say plainly when it does not know.

The best early sign is quiet. Routine questions stop landing on you, and the tricky ones reach you with enough detail that you can act without calling back to ask what the customer meant.

Where a person takes over

Every receptionist service needs a clear handoff. Anything about money, refunds, complaints, legal questions or a caller who sounds upset should go to you. Ask who decides what counts as tricky, and whether you can change that list yourself after launch.

Ground rules we hold to for every helper we set up: it tells callers it is an AI, it never asks for your passwords, it only reads the information you point it at, you can type PAUSE and it stops, and it does not give legal, tax or financial advice.

Setup matters more than the tool

Two services can use similar technology and give very different results. The difference is usually the setup: which questions it answers, what it says when it is unsure, and what it does after hours. A planning call should cover your hours, your most common questions, the jobs you never want handled without you, and what you need to see before a message goes out.

For a receptionist-style job, the Customer Questions Agent in our plans answers common questions from your own information and hands the tricky ones to you. You decide what goes into its information, and you can see what it was given.

Questions to ask a provider before you buy

  1. Who writes the answers the receptionist gives, and can I review them before it goes live?
  2. What happens when a caller asks something that is not on the list I approved?
  3. How does a person take over, and how quickly will I see the message?
  4. Will it tell callers it is an AI when they ask?
  5. Who owns the account and the information it uses, and can I take it with me if I leave?
  6. What do I approve, and what runs on its own?

What to expect in the first stretch

The first stretch is the busiest, because the helper meets questions you did not think to include. That is useful information, not a failure. Keep a short list of anything it got wrong or could not answer, and bring that list to your check-ins so the answers get better over time.

Compare that with the end state you want: a short daily summary you read in a few minutes, with the messages that need you already flagged. (Our receptionist covers website chat, text and email; live phone answering is not part of our plans today.) A provider who cannot describe the path from the first week to that summary has not thought through the setup.

Common questions

How much does an AI receptionist service cost?

The cost depends on the plan you pick and how much setup your business needs. Ask any provider what the monthly price covers, what setup includes, and what you pay separately. Our current plans are listed on the packages page, and you need your own AI account, which is paid separately.

Can an AI receptionist answer the phone for my business?

It can answer questions it has been given information for, take messages and hand a call to a person, depending on how it is set up. It should tell callers it is an AI when they ask. It should not promise anything you have not approved, and it should not give legal, tax or financial advice.

When should a person take over from an AI receptionist?

A person should take over for complaints, refunds, pricing exceptions, legal or medical questions, and any caller who is upset or asks for a human. Agree on that list before setup, and review it once you have seen the first few weeks of messages.
