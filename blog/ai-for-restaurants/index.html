AI for restaurants: reservations, reviews and event posts

Ward Consulting AI · November 11, 2026

AI for restaurants usually means helpers that answer the questions guests ask over and over, draft review replies and write event posts, while you approve every word before it goes out. For a small restaurant, the value sits in the routine work that eats the hours between services, not in replacing the people who cook and serve.

Where small restaurants start

Guests ask the same questions every week: your hours, parking, whether you take walk-ins, whether the patio is open, what is in a dish. Reservation and catering questions often arrive late at night or during a rush, when nobody can type a careful answer. A helper can handle the questions you have already answered, and hand over everything else.

Start with the question that costs you the most. If the phone rings during service and nobody answers, the reservation questions may matter most. If Monday mornings disappear into writing posts, the event notes may matter more. Name the one before you talk to any provider.

For example, a family restaurant might start with questions about hours and private rooms. A food truck that takes catering requests might start with quote follow-ups. Both examples describe the kind of work involved, not results you should expect.

Review replies you approve

Reviews are public, so every reply should be read by you before it posts. A good draft thanks the guest, addresses the specific point and avoids arguing. It should not promise a refund, a discount or a return visit on your behalf. Any reply that mentions a comp, a refund, a named staff member or a health concern should wait for you, every time.

Reply within a day when you can, because a reply that arrives weeks later reads as an afterthought. Ask how the drafts are written. You want a voice that sounds like your restaurant, not a template that could belong to any place on the same street.

Event posts and catering seasons

Restaurants with trivia nights, live music, holiday menus or a busy catering season always have more to post than time to write. A helper can draft posts and newsletter lines from a short note you leave, such as trivia moving to a new night or a new seasonal soup. You approve the wording, the date and any price before anything goes out.

Keep the notes short and dated. A line like trivia moved to Thursday this month is enough. The helper works from what you give it, so a vague note produces a vague post.

Catering is a good place to be strict. Quotes, deposits and delivery windows are money and commitments, so they stay with you. A helper can remind you that a quote is waiting, and it can draft the follow-up, but it should not send a price on its own.

Questions to ask a provider before you pay

  1. When a guest asks something the helper cannot answer, how does it reach a person, and how fast?
  2. Who approves a review reply before it posts publicly?
  3. Can the helper see reservation details, and what is it never allowed to touch?
  4. How are menu, price and allergy questions handled, and who confirms them?
  5. What happens to our information and our accounts if we stop?

The allergy question deserves a direct answer. A helper should draw only from information you have approved, should never give medical advice, and should hand any question about a health concern to a person who knows the kitchen.

Signs a restaurant is ready for a setup

  • You already have written answers to the questions guests ask most.
  • You can name one slow, routine task, such as review replies or event posts.
  • Someone on your team will read a short daily check and decide what to approve.

Common questions

How can AI help a restaurant with reservations?

An AI helper can answer questions about hours, parking, seating and catering from information you provide, and it can hand reservation changes or unusual requests to your team. It should not confirm a booking you have not approved, and it should say it is an AI when a guest asks.

Should AI write my restaurant's review replies?

It can draft them, but you should approve every reply before it posts, because reviews are public. Good drafts thank the guest, address the specific point and avoid arguing. Anything that mentions a refund, a comp or a health concern should come to you first.

Can AI handle allergy and menu questions for a restaurant?

Only from the information you have approved, and never as medical advice. Anything about allergies should be confirmed by a person who knows the kitchen. Ask any provider how menu and allergen questions are handled before you agree to anything.
