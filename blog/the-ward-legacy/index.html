The Ward legacy: from a grocery-store demo to AI helpers

Ward Consulting AI · November 10, 2026

Ward Consulting AI is the newest branch of a family business that started in 1971, when David Ward mixed his own lens cleaner and sold it one demonstration at a time. His grandson, Chris Ward, runs it today from the same Ogden, Utah address as Z-Clear Anti-Fog. This is the story of how a grocery-store demo turned into the way we set up AI for other owners.

Where the Ward legacy starts

Z-Clear began in the small town of Malad, Idaho. David Ward came up with a lens cleaning and anti-fog recipe he believed beat anything on the store shelf.

He did not buy advertising. He went to the local grocery store, set up, and showed people what it did. On plenty of those days his kids came along to help. Once people tried it, they kept buying it.

That was 1971. Z-Clear is still family-owned, now in its third generation, and still mixed by hand in small batches in Ogden. You can read the full founding story on the Z-Clear blog.

What a grocery-store demo taught us about AI

A demo is an honest way to sell. The customer sees the thing work, on their own glasses, before they pay. Nothing is hidden behind a brochure.

We try to sell AI the same way. Chris owns and runs several businesses, so every helper we offer runs first in his own companies:

  • Z-Clear Anti-Fog, the family company David started.
  • GoodLife Nailz, a nail salon in North Ogden's Cannery Center.
  • GoodLife Gemz, a crystal shop in the same building.
  • A snow removal business, which runs on short notice and early mornings.

Only once a helper has done real work in those businesses do we offer it to anyone else. That is our version of standing at the grocery store with a bottle and a cloth.

Two helpers that already work in the family business

At Z-Clear, an AI helper reads the inbox first every morning and flags what needs a person. Another shops hotel rooms and rental cars for event trips, then re-checks the bookings before free cancellation runs out.

Neither one spends money or sends anything on its own. They draft, they flag, and a person decides. That rule came from running a family company, where one careless message to a long-time customer costs more than any tool saves.

If you own a salon, a shop or a service business, those are the same kinds of tasks that eat your mornings. Our guide to AI agents for small business covers the common ones.

What working with a family business looks like

Most of the owners we talk to run something small and personal. Their name is on it, or their parents' name. They are not looking for a tech project. They want the busywork handled without losing the personal touch their customers come back for.

Here is what to expect when you work with us:

  1. Talk. A free call about your busiest job and what slows you down.
  2. Map. We draw that job as a workflow: what starts it, each step, who approves, and what "done" looks like.
  3. Set up. Your helpers go into your own Grok account, starting in test mode, writing drafts only.
  4. You approve. Anything that spends money or can't be undone waits for your yes. Silence is never a yes.
  5. Daily check. You see whether each helper ran, what it made, and what is waiting for you.

Every helper says it's AI, never asks for your passwords, only reads what you point it at, and stops when you type PAUSE. If you run a salon, our page on AI for salons shows how this fits a booking-driven business.
